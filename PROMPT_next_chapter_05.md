The structural shape of the chapter, per the outline:

1. Brief framing: the framework has been defined (Chapter 4). Now apply it. Two systems, four axes each.

2. Industrial feedlot animal production, scored axis by axis:
   - Economic: positive short-term (cheap meat at point of sale); negative when externalities are accounted (healthcare costs, pollution remediation, antibiotic resistance economic impact, rural community disinvestment).
   - Social: net negative (worker conditions, community health impacts, displacement of family farmers).
   - Environmental: net negative (waste lagoons, water pollution, antibiotic resistance, GHG emissions, no carbon sequestration).
   - Survivability: very low. Requires continuous commodity grain inputs, continuous federal subsidies for feed corn, continuous diesel for transportation, continuous antibiotics for animal health. Remove any one and the system fails.

3. Industrial grain monoculture, scored axis by axis:
   - Economic: positive for centralized producers; negative for local economies and family farmers.
   - Social: net negative for rural communities; positive for urban consumers in the form of cheap calories.
   - Environmental: net negative (topsoil loss, biodiversity reduction, groundwater contamination, mycorrhizal damage from fungicides, runoff as the leading cause of non-point source water pollution in the US).
   - Survivability: very low. Requires continuous chemical inputs, continuous fossil fuel for tractors and irrigation pumps, continuous federal subsidies, continuous favorable climate for predictable yields. Remove any one and the system fails.
