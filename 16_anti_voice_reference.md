# Anti-Voice Reference: Patterns to Suppress

**Purpose:** This document catalogs specific voice patterns from the 2012 manuscript that are not to appear in the new book under any circumstances. It is the negative companion to the voice notes (which catalog positive patterns to emulate from the 2015 and 2016 essays).

**How to use this file:** Before drafting any chapter, scan this list. After drafting, scan the draft against this list as a compliance check. Any pattern listed here that appears in a draft is a blocking issue — the draft does not advance to review until the pattern is removed.

**Source:** The 2012 manuscript (`02_archive_2012_manuscript_part1.md`, `03_archive_2012_manuscript_part2.md`, `04_archive_2012_manuscript_part3.md`). Specific examples below are drawn from those files. Do not emulate any of them. They are included only so the patterns are unambiguous.

---

## I. Reader-Hectoring Patterns

The 2012 manuscript treats the reader as an adversary to be cornered. The new book treats the reader as a thinking adult to be addressed. The following patterns are forbidden.

### "Write that down"

The 2012 manuscript uses "write that down" or close variants ("get a tattoo," "tell a friend," "remember that") at least a dozen times across the three parts. Every instance is a moment where the author distrusts the reader to retain information without being commanded to.

**Do not use:**
- "Write that down."
- "Write *that* down."
- "Get a tattoo."
- "Tell a friend."
- "Remember that."
- "Don't forget."
- Any variant in which the author commands the reader to record, remember, or share a claim.

**Why:** The reader will retain what is well-argued. Commands to retain are a tell that the author doesn't trust the argument.

### Second-person accusation and assumption

The 2012 manuscript routinely puts beliefs and behaviors into the reader's mouth in order to attack them.

**Do not use constructions like:**
- "You think X, and you're wrong because…"
- "If you're a vegetarian reading this (which is admittedly unlikely)…"
- "You probably bought into that load of crap because…"
- "Maybe you're one of those people who…"
- "You silly, you're the taxpayer."

**Why:** The reader becomes a hostile character in their own reading experience. They close the book.

### Direct insult of the reader

The 2012 manuscript at multiple points calls the reader, or hypothetical readers in the reader's category, idiots, jerks, suckers, morons, or assholes.

**Forbidden under all circumstances:**
- Any direct or indirect address that calls the reader (or any class of reader the actual reader might belong to) an insulting name.
- "Asshole," "idiot," "moron," "jerk," "sucker," "lovepuff," and similar.

**Why:** This is non-negotiable. There is no version of the new book in which any reader is insulted.

### "Duh"

Used over a dozen times in the 2012 manuscript. Every instance signals that the author finds the point obvious and the reader stupid for needing it explained.

**Do not use:** "Duh." "Hello." "C'mon." "Get a clue." "No shit." "Capisce?" or any rhetorical move that signals the author's impatience with the reader's understanding.

## II. Certainty-Performance Patterns

The 2012 manuscript performs certainty it cannot earn. The new book argues for what it can defend and acknowledges what it cannot. The following patterns are forbidden because they substitute volume for evidence.

### All-caps emphasis

The 2012 manuscript uses ALL CAPS for emphasis dozens of times across the three parts, often for entire phrases or sentences.

**Do not use:**
- All-caps words for emphasis ("we are creating antibiotic RESISTANT bacteria")
- All-caps phrases ("HO. LEE. SHIT.")
- All-caps headers used to emphasize rather than to label
- Repeated bold sentences used to compensate for an under-argued claim

**Acceptable:** Italics for genuine emphasis, used sparingly. Headers for navigation, not for shouting.

### Repeated assertion as substitute for argument

The 2012 manuscript at one point states "Biological diversity is a global resource that needs to be preserved" six times in a row, in bold, then explicitly notes the repetition. This is the pattern in extreme form. It appears in milder forms throughout.

**Do not use:**
- The same sentence repeated for emphasis
- The same sentence in bold for emphasis
- "I said it three times so you'd remember"
- Any structural move where repetition substitutes for evidence

**Why:** A claim that needs to be repeated has not been demonstrated. Demonstrate it instead.

### Conclusory rhetoric without warrant

The 2012 manuscript routinely concludes with phrases like "fail," "win," "the end," "case closed," "argument won," when the argument has not actually been concluded.

**Do not use:**
- "Fail."
- "Win."
- "Win, win."
- "Game over."
- "Case in point: [claim]. The end."
- "Argument over."
- "I rest my case."
- Any verbal flourish that asserts the author has won an exchange the reader is still evaluating.

### "Bullshit" as analytical category

The 2012 manuscript uses "bullshit" as the primary verdict on opposing positions, often as the substantive engagement rather than as a colorful supplement to it.

**Do not use:** "Bullshit," "BS," "load of crap," "load of shit," "complete and utter [X]," or similar, as a substitute for actual analysis. If a claim is wrong, demonstrate where and why. Profanity is acceptable in the new book in moderation; profanity-as-argument is not.

## III. Adversarial-Posture Patterns

The 2012 manuscript identifies enemies and attacks them. The new book steelmans interlocutors and engages them. The following patterns are forbidden because they encode the adversarial posture into the prose.

### Personal name-calling

The 2012 manuscript repeatedly refers to public figures by insulting nicknames or with personal hostility. Examples: Tom Vilsack as "Shitsack" and "Tailored," Kraft Foods executives as "bastards," Colin Campbell as "a sexually suppressed nutjob himself like his predecessors," Sylvester Graham as "a guy who was anti-getting down with the get down."

**Forbidden:**
- Insulting nicknames for any public figure
- Personal characterizations of public figures (their sexuality, their intelligence, their character) as analytical content
- Gratuitous biographical attack
- Wishing harm or misfortune on any person, even rhetorically ("I hope you get strangled by a stethoscope")

**Acceptable:** Substantive critique of a public figure's positions, decisions, or institutional role, with their name used neutrally and their position represented in its strongest form before being engaged.

### Group attack on identity-based categories

The 2012 manuscript attacks vegetarians, vegans, "Vegangelicals," "PETA fans," "smug vegetarian Prius drivers," and similar groups as categories.

**Forbidden:**
- Generalized attack on any dietary, political, or lifestyle group
- Mockery of group members for their group membership
- Constructions like "those people," "these people," "PETA fans," etc., used dismissively
- The asterisked obscuring of "v\*getarian" or any equivalent bit

**Acceptable:** Engagement with specific arguments made by specific writers within a tradition. Acknowledgment that members of any dietary tradition are autonomous adults making informed choices.

### "I hate them back"

The 2012 manuscript at multiple points expresses personal hatred toward groups or individuals. "PETA hates Paleo people. So what, I hate them back."

**Forbidden:** Any expression of personal hatred, disdain, or contempt toward any person or group. The new book's posture is curiosity and disagreement, never hatred.

### Mockery of the disabled, the dead, or the absent

The 2012 manuscript at points mocks people who cannot defend themselves: dead writers, disabled people implicitly grouped with "diseased" populations, public figures who haven't been engaged for response.

**Forbidden:** Any mockery of any person who cannot reply. If a critique is worth making, it is worth making to the person's face — meaning, in writing they could read and respond to.

## IV. Tone-Drift Patterns

The 2012 manuscript shifts register constantly — from analytical to combative to scatological to confessional to commanding — within single paragraphs. The new book maintains a stable register: warm, then direct, then precise. The following patterns are forbidden because they break that register.

### Scatological humor

Extensive use of "shit," "shit-lagoon," "shitsack," "diarrhea," "projectile diarrhea," "shit-traffic," "shit-geyser," and related. Some is descriptive of CAFO waste lagoons (which is legitimate); most is for shock or laugh.

**Acceptable:** Descriptive use where the substance is literally what is being described (waste lagoons, manure handling).

**Not acceptable:** Scatological vocabulary used for emphasis, humor, or as general intensifier ("that's some shitty thinking," "shitsack," etc.).

### Sexual humor and innuendo

The 2012 manuscript has extended sections about sex drive, "getting laid," aphrodisiacs, "penis opening" jokes, and "lol" reactions to fetal abnormality data.

**Forbidden:**
- Any sexual humor
- Any innuendo
- Any "lol" or comedic framing of biological harm data, especially birth defects or developmental abnormalities
- The Sylvester Graham extended bit about masturbation suppression
- The "look at the DuranRider guy" appearance-mockery
- Any move that treats sex, attraction, or reproductive biology as material for jokes

### Pop-culture asides as analytical content

The 2012 manuscript leans heavily on Walking Dead, Hunger Games, Lion King, Princess Bride, Hook, Ghostbusters, Stay-Puft Marshmallow Man, and similar references, often as the central frame for an argument rather than as occasional flavor.

**Acceptable:** Occasional, well-chosen reference as illustration, when the reference is doing genuine work and would land for readers in 2026 and 2036.

**Not acceptable:**
- Extended argument structured around a TV show or film
- References that date the book to a specific year (Walking Dead's cultural moment was 2011-2014)
- Multiple pop-culture references in close proximity
- Pop-culture references as a substitute for citation or argument

### George Carlin monologue-quotation

The 2012 manuscript at one point quotes approximately 800 words of a George Carlin monologue. This is both a copyright issue and a tonal one — the Carlin voice subsumes the author's voice for the duration.

**Forbidden:**
- Extended quotation of any comedian or pop figure
- Use of someone else's voice to deliver the book's argument
- Reliance on borrowed personality for emphasis

### "Honey badger" / animal-mascot self-references

The 2012 manuscript refers to the author as "Original Paleo Honey Badger" and uses "OPHB" as a signature. The new book has no mascot, no nickname, no in-group signaling.

**Forbidden:** Any cute self-reference, mascot, in-joke, or signaling that addresses an in-group at the expense of clarity to the general reader.

## V. Structural Patterns to Suppress

These are not voice patterns exactly, but structural habits that produce the 2012 voice and must be broken.

### Sidebar boxes labeled with cute titles

The 2012 manuscript uses "Little Box of Common Sense," "Box of Sciency Shit," "Quick Aside," "Total Aside," "Tell a Friend," and similar named sidebars. The labels are jokes; the sidebars often contain the same content as the main text.

**Acceptable in the new book:** Genuinely supplementary material in clearly labeled boxes (e.g., "Background: The Younger Dryas Hypothesis"), used sparingly.

**Not acceptable:** Joke-titled sidebars. Sidebars that duplicate main-text content. Sidebars used for the author to step out of the argument and address the reader sideways.

### Self-interrupting parentheticals

The 2012 manuscript constantly steps out of its own sentences to comment on them. "(lol)", "(I'm vain like that)", "(haha, I said gaping)", "(that's me, btw)".

**Forbidden:** Any parenthetical that breaks the fourth wall to comment on the author's own writing or to insert a reaction to the author's own claim.

**Acceptable:** Parentheticals that add genuine clarifying information.

### "And by X, I mean X"

The 2012 manuscript uses this construction repeatedly: "by science, I specifically mean…", "by resistant I mean resistant," "and by active, I mean eat Paleo."

**Acceptable in moderation:** When it's genuinely clarifying a term.

**Not acceptable:** When it's a verbal tic that adds no information.

### The sing-song "fail" / "win" structure at section ends

The 2012 manuscript ends sections with "Fail.", "Win, win.", "BioDiversity: 1, Monocultures, 0.", "Mother Nature: 3, Mexicans 0.", and similar score-keeping flourishes.

**Forbidden:** All of it. Sections end with the next thought, not a victory lap.

## VI. Specific Phrases and Tics — Forbidden List

The following words and phrases are not to appear in the new book under any circumstances:

- "Write that down" (and variants)
- "Duh"
- "Get a clue"
- "Slow your role"
- "Capisce"
- "Lovepuff"
- "Honey badger" (in self-reference)
- "Vegangelical"
- "Shitsack" (or any insulting nickname for a real person)
- "Friggin'" (as register marker)
- "WTF" (or spelled out)
- "Holy shit" (as transition or emphasis)
- "Jerk," "asshole," "moron," "idiot," "sucker" — applied to any reader, group, or named person
- "Tofurkey" (as dismissive)
- "Smug vegetarian"
- "Anthropocentric bitch" (in self-reference)
- Any all-caps phrase or sentence
- "v\*getarian" or any asterisked obscuring of words

## VII. What Remains

The 2012 manuscript is not entirely without value. The following elements survive into the new book in transformed form, but the surrounding voice does not:

- The factual accounting of CAFO externalities (numbers updated, citations verified)
- The Salatin feed math (kept exactly, presented without hostility)
- The Zombie Apocalypse / survivability framework (renamed, formalized)
- The structural argument that "sustainable" operations can be tethered to industrial inputs
- The personal narrative of recognizing label inadequacy (substantially rewritten)
- The acknowledgment that something must die to feed us (kept, expressed without cruelty toward those who feel otherwise)
- The interest in Paul S. Martin's work (becomes the book's spine)

These elements are extracted as content. They do not bring the 2012 voice with them.

## Compliance Process

1. Before drafting any chapter, read this file in full.
2. After drafting, search the draft for each item in Section VI as a literal text search.
3. Read the draft once specifically scanning for Section I-V patterns.
4. Any match found is flagged and removed before the draft advances to review.
5. If a pattern in this file appears in three consecutive drafts, the voice notes are revised to make the suppression more explicit.
