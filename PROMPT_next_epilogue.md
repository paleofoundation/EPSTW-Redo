The structural shape of the epilogue, per the outline:

1. Return to the orchard image. By this point the reader has been through the entire book. The orchard — camels, osage oranges, the fruit with a mouth — has accumulated meaning it did not have in the Prologue. Let that accumulated meaning do the work.

2. Acknowledgment of what the book has not solved. The book has made an argument that is partial, contested, and dependent on conditions the author does not control. The epilogue is not triumphant. It is honest.

3. The honest position: the framework is a proposal. The rewilding case is a hypothesis worth testing. The work continues.

4. Final image: the orchard, still hypothetical, still possible. The longing named plainly, as it was in the Prologue, but now carrying the weight of everything between.

What the epilogue does NOT do:

- Is not triumphant. The book has not won an argument; it has made one.
- Does not summarize the book. The reader has just read it.
- Does not introduce new arguments or new material.
- Does not end with a rallying cry or a call to action (that was Chapter 13's job).
- Does not end with a quotation or epigraph (the misattributed "We Do Not Inherit the Earth" quote was cut per the README's resolved decisions).

The epilogue ends with a question the reader can sit with, not an answer the reader is expected to accept.
