## What This Book Is

This book is a tribute to Paul S. Martin's rewilding vision, made accessible to general readers who will not otherwise encounter it. *Twilight of the Mammoths* (2005) is the intellectual headwater. The book's job is to put Martin's reframing of "native," his Pleistocene baseline, and his rewilding proposal back into circulation for a generation that needs them.

The author is the lens. Martin is the protagonist of the ideas.

## What This Book Is Not

- Not a Paleo diet book. The author has revised significantly on this since 2012; microbiome individuality means there is no universal human diet.
- Not a polemic against vegetarians, Salatin, Savory, Pollan, or any other figure in the food-systems conversation. Disagreement where warranted, in good faith, with intellectual fairness.
- Not a takedown of monocultures categorically. There is room for monoculture improvement; the world is changing rapidly; backward is not the only direction.
- Not a personal brand vehicle. No "Pendergrass Score." The framework, if named, is named descriptively.
- Not a money-making project. The book exists because it should exist.

## Core Thesis

The dominant framework for evaluating food system sustainability rests on three axes — economic, social, environmental — that describe a system's present state under stable conditions. A fourth axis, **survivability**, describes how the system behaves when conditions change: when subsidies end, when commodity inputs become expensive, when supply chains break, when climate volatility intensifies. Most food systems that score well on the three traditional axes score poorly on survivability. Most systems that would score well on survivability are excluded from current sustainability conversations.

Pleistocene rewilding — the use of phylogenetically appropriate proxies to restore ecological function lost in the late-Pleistocene extinctions — scores well on all four axes and represents the most underexplored direction in food-systems thinking. The book makes the case for testing it.

## Authorial Posture

- **Open to being wrong.** The author has changed her mind on major points over sixteen years and the book reflects that. Where the author previously argued the opposite, she says so.
- **Asking for proof of concept, not certainty.** Critics have legitimate concerns. The book does not decry them. It asks for room to test the ideas.
- **Crediting predecessors generously.** Martin, Donlan, Savory, Tree, Monbiot (where credit is due), Ridley (for the monoculture-improvement revision), and others get full and fair acknowledgment.
- **Steelmanning before critique.** Every figure or position critiqued in the book is presented in its strongest formulation first.

## Voice

First-person. Warm-then-direct-then-precise. More formal than the author's spoken register, less formal than academic prose. Closer to long-form essay than blog post. Specific, concrete, and willing to use the word "I" without performing intimacy.

The author's revised intellectual position is itself part of the story. Self-revision is treated as evidence of seriousness, not weakness.

## Working Agreement

- Disagreements are surfaced directly. Pendergrass tells Claude when something is wrong.

## Resolved Decisions (2026-04-16)

These were the original "Open Questions for Pendergrass." All four are now resolved.

- **Camelops opener epigraph — resolved.** The "We Do Not Inherit the Earth from Our Ancestors..." quote is cut. It is widely misattributed (variously credited to a Native American proverb, Chief Seattle, Wendell Berry, and Antoine de Saint-Exupéry) and its provenance does not hold up under the book's citation standard. The prologue will open with something either original to Karen or cleanly attributable to a real, citable source. Claude to propose at prologue-drafting stage.

- **Savory food-truck scene placement — resolved.** Primary placement is Chapter 7 (*Wrong Proxy, Right Place*), where it does the heaviest argumentative work: bridging from regenerative agriculture's limits into the proxy-selection question. A brief echo in the prologue is permitted as one of the "this is where the book came from" beats if that serves prologue momentum. Final call during drafting.

- **Martin permissions — resolved.** Engage the estate / University of Arizona Press. Authorized homage reads differently than fair-use commentary, and engagement opens doors (Martin's Arizona colleagues, coauthors, rewilding researchers he mentored) that fair use does not. Outreach should happen *early*, before deep drafting on the Martin-heavy chapters (Prologue, Ch. 3 *Near Time*, Ch. 7 *Wrong Proxy Right Place*), so the conversation can shape the work rather than merely review it. Action: Claude flags Martin-heavy passages and keeps a running list of Martin quotations with page numbers to support the permissions conversation.

- **Names to exclude — resolved.** Authoritative list lives at `15_exclusion_list.md`. Excluded from the book in any capacity: Elke Nelson, Mat Lalonde, any David Pendergrass. Cleared for inclusion where editorially relevant: Robb Wolf, Allan Savory, Paul S. Martin, Diana Rodgers (RD), George Monbiot, Michael Pollan, Marion Nestle. Additional exclusions can be added at any time by naming the person; no rationale required.

## Additional Standing Rule (2026-04-16)

**Every scientific claim requires a citation and a real footnote to a primary or peer-reviewed source.** No "studies show" hand-waving. No citing popular books when the underlying peer-reviewed source is available. If a primary source cannot be found, the claim is rewritten as framing/narrative or cut. Central register is `13_source_bibliography.md`. This standard applies from the first draft onward — it will slow drafting slightly and make the book significantly more defensible.
